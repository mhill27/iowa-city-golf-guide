Beginner Golf Tips: Learn to Play Golf

New to golf? This beginner golf guide covers equipment, practice routines, and basic etiquette to help you feel more comfortable learning the game in Iowa City.

Five Steps Before Your First Round

Learning to play golf can feel overwhelming at first, but you do not need expensive equipment or a perfect swing to get started. These five beginner golf tips can make your first experience easier.

01 / Equipment

Keep Your Equipment Simple

You do not need fourteen clubs when starting out. A putter, wedge, iron, and longer club are enough to begin practicing. Consider borrowing or renting clubs before purchasing a full set.

02 / Practice

Start With Short Shots

Good golf training for beginners starts with putting and chipping. Focus on making consistent contact rather than trying to hit the ball as far as possible.

03 / Etiquette

Learn Basic Golf Etiquette

Stay quiet while other golfers hit, repair ball marks when appropriate, and respect the people around you. Learning basic etiquette makes your first round more enjoyable.

04 / Pace

Keep the Game Moving

Consider starting with nine holes instead of eighteen. If a hole is taking too long, it is okay to pick up your ball and move to the next one during a casual round.

05 / Confidence

Choose a Beginner-Friendly Course

When learning how to play golf, the course you choose can make a big difference. A shorter or less challenging layout can help you enjoy the game without feeling rushed.

A Simple Golf Practice Routine for Beginners

If you're looking for a basic golf tutorial for beginners, start with this practice routine. You can try it at a driving range or practice facility.

  • Spend ten minutes practicing short putts.
  • Hit a few chip shots with a wedge.
  • Practice controlled half-swings with a short iron.
  • Focus on making solid contact instead of hitting for distance.
  • Take breaks and finish with a few more putts.

Golf Terms Every Beginner Should Know

Golf has a lot of terms that can be confusing when you're first starting out. Here are nine common words you'll hear on the course.

Par

The number of strokes a skilled golfer is expected to take to complete a hole.

Birdie

Finishing a hole one stroke under par.

Bogey

Finishing a hole one stroke over par.

Double Bogey

Finishing a hole two strokes over par.

Fairway

The shorter grass between the teeing area and the putting green.

Green

The closely cut grass around the hole where golfers normally putt.

Handicap

A number used to represent a golfer's demonstrated playing ability.

Tee Time

The scheduled time when your group is supposed to start its round.

Driving Range

A practice area where golfers hit balls to work on their golf swings.
