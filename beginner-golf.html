Beginner guide

New to golf? Start here.

Everything you need to feel more comfortable at a practice range and during your first round, without getting lost in technical terms.

Five steps before your first round

01 / Gear

Keep equipment simple

You do not need fourteen clubs immediately. A putter, a wedge, an iron, and a longer club are enough to begin learning. Borrow or rent when possible.

02 / Practice

Start with short shots

Practice putting and small chips before focusing on full swings. Consistent contact matters more than hitting the ball a long distance.

03 / Etiquette

Respect the group

Stay quiet when someone is hitting, watch for other golfers, repair marks when appropriate, and be ready when it is your turn.

04 / Pace

Keep things moving

For a casual learning round, consider nine holes. If a hole is taking too long, it is fine to pick up your ball and move along.

05 / Confidence

Choose the right setting

A shorter, beginner-friendly practice experience is often more fun than jumping straight into a demanding full-length layout. See our fictional course comparisons to understand the options.

A straightforward first practice session

  • Spend ten minutes putting from short distances.
  • Hit a few chip shots with a wedge.
  • Try controlled half-swings with a short iron.
  • Take breaks, focus on making contact, and finish with a few putts.
